## Data
Intake session. Deborah is 44 and about eighteen months into a blended household following her 2023 marriage to Marcus. The home now includes her son Jamie, from her earlier relationship with her ex-partner Ray, and Marcus's two children, Chloe and Ethan. She described a steady drumbeat of [[Family-Conflict]] over bedtimes, screens, and whose rules apply to whom, and named ongoing co-parenting stress with Ray as the thing that "sets the whole week on edge." She said she often feels like "the switchboard everyone shouts through." Risk was screened explicitly at intake; Deborah clearly denied any thoughts of self-harm, suicide, or wanting to be dead, and reported no such history. She presented as organized, self-aware, and tired.

## Assessment
Presentation fits a relational adjustment picture (Z63.8) rather than an individual disorder: the distress lives in the household structure. I introduced the family systems frame, explaining that recurring conflict usually reflects roles and positions rather than any one person's failing. I began light [[genogram-mapping]], sketching the two households and the children's movement between them. Deborah leaned in immediately, saying "seeing it on paper already makes it less about me being a bad stepmother." That response signaled strong engagement and supports early progress on the v1 goal of understanding the family map.

## Plan
Agreed a roughly five to six week cadence given scheduling across two households. Asked Deborah to notice this week which conflicts recur and who tends to get pulled in. Will continue building the genogram next session.

## Next Session Considerations
- Consider extending the genogram back a generation to Deborah's family of origin.
- Possible focus on naming the most frequent conflict flashpoints.

Clinical judgment and final session planning remain the therapist's responsibility.
