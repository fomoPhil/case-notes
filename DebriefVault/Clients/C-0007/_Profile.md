Aisha is a 31 year old woman who entered treatment in June 2026 with persistent fear of having a serious undiagnosed illness. She describes catastrophic misinterpretation of benign bodily sensations, a rapid heartbeat becomes evidence of cardiac disease, a headache becomes a possible tumor. Her distress connects strongly to [[Health-Anxiety]] and a broader pattern of [[Anxiety]] that predates the current episode. She reports frequent body scanning, repeated online symptom checking, and seeking reassurance from doctors and family members, all of which lower her fear briefly before it returns stronger.

Treatment uses cognitive behavioral therapy. Early sessions focused on psychoeducation about the health anxiety cycle and on building a shared formulation that frames her checking, googling, and reassurance seeking as safety behaviors that maintain rather than resolve the fear. She engaged readily with the model and could name the short term relief and long term cost of these behaviors.

As a newer client she is early in treatment and still building confidence in the approach. Progress so far is modest but genuine: she has completed her first thought records on catastrophic health thoughts and has begun a response prevention plan to reduce checking and reassurance seeking. Gains are fragile and she remains prone to relapse into checking under stress. Risk was screened at intake and denied, with no current risk flags.
