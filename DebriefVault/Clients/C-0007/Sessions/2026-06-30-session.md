## Data
Aisha returned having tracked her worries over the fortnight. She reported a spike two days earlier when a tension headache led her to spend an hour online searching brain tumor symptoms, followed by texting her sister for reassurance. She described the familiar arc of brief relief and returning dread. Her account continued to center on [[Health-Anxiety]] and its overlap with generalized [[Anxiety]]. She named her main safety behaviors clearly: body scanning, online symptom checking, and reassurance seeking from family and doctors.

## Assessment
The session focused on connecting benign sensations to their catastrophic interpretations. I introduced [[thought-records]] and we completed one together for the headache episode. Her catastrophic thought, "this headache means something is seriously wrong with my brain," carried an initial belief rating of 80 percent. Using [[cognitive-restructuring]] we examined evidence for and against and generated a balanced alternative referencing stress and screen time. Belief fell to 45 percent by the end. She remarked, "I can see the other explanation, but it still feels risky to trust it," which reflects genuine but partial cognitive shift and steady progress on Goal 1. The residual pull toward certainty remains a barrier.

## Plan
Assigned two thought records before the next session, one for a body sensation and one for a checking urge. Asked her to also log daily frequency of scanning, googling, and reassurance seeking to feed Goal 2.

## Next Session Considerations
- Consider beginning response prevention for one checking behavior.
- Possible focus on the short term relief versus long term cost of reassurance seeking.

Clinical judgment and final session planning remain the therapist's responsibility.
