## Data
Intake session. Aisha is a 31 year old woman presenting with an intense fear of having a serious undiagnosed illness. She described months of worry that ordinary bodily sensations signal disease, for example reading a fast heartbeat as a sign of heart trouble. She linked her distress to [[Health-Anxiety]] and a longstanding tendency toward [[Anxiety]]. She reported frequent body scanning, repeated online symptom checking, and reassurance seeking from her GP and family, stating "if I can just be sure nothing is wrong, I can relax, but it never lasts." Risk was screened explicitly at intake and Aisha denied any thoughts of self-harm, suicide, or wanting to be dead. She presented as cooperative, articulate, and relieved to name the pattern.

## Assessment
Presentation is consistent with illness anxiety disorder (F45.21). Benign interoceptive sensations are subject to catastrophic misinterpretation, and her checking, googling, and reassurance seeking function as safety behaviors that lower anxiety briefly while reinforcing the underlying threat belief. I delivered psychoeducation on the health anxiety cycle and drew a shared formulation linking trigger sensation, catastrophic thought, anxiety spike, and safety behavior. Using [[cognitive-restructuring]] I gently separated one specific sensation from the feared meaning she assigned it. She responded, "so the checking is actually feeding it," which showed good insight and supported early engagement on Goal 1.

## Plan
Provided a handout on the health anxiety maintenance cycle. Asked Aisha to notice, without changing anything yet, when body scanning and symptom checking occur before the next session. Agreed to a biweekly cadence.

## Next Session Considerations
- Consider introducing a first thought record for a catastrophic health thought.
- Possible focus on mapping her main safety behaviors in detail.

Clinical judgment and final session planning remain the therapist's responsibility.
