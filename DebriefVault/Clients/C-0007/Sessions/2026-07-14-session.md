## Data
Aisha brought two completed thought records and her frequency log, which showed online symptom checking most days and reassurance texts to her sister three or four times a week. She noted a clearer awareness of her attentional bias, describing how body scanning primes her to find sensations she then misreads. Her focus remained on [[Health-Anxiety]] alongside underlying [[Anxiety]]. She said she felt ready to try changing a behavior rather than only observing it.

## Assessment
We reframed reducing safety behaviors as [[graded-exposure]], with response prevention as the mechanism: tolerating the urge to check without acting maintains contact with uncertainty until anxiety falls on its own. We agreed on a first step of delaying online symptom checking by 30 minutes and dropping one daily reassurance text. She practiced the delay in session with a live urge and reported her anxiety rose then eased without checking, saying "it peaked and then it actually came down by itself." This concrete in-session shift supports progress on Goal 2. Using her [[thought-records]] we also drafted a first behavioral experiment to test whether not googling a mild symptom leads to the feared outcome. Fear of a missed diagnosis remains the main barrier.

## Plan
Agreed to a graded response prevention plan for the fortnight: delay then reduce symptom checking, and cut reassurance seeking to once weekly. Asked her to run the planned behavioral experiment once and record predicted versus actual outcome.

## Next Session Considerations
- Consider reviewing the behavioral experiment results and updating beliefs.
- Possible focus on extending response prevention to body scanning.

Clinical judgment and final session planning remain the therapist's responsibility.
