Eleanor is a 52-year-old woman who came to therapy following the recent death of her mother, carrying a longstanding sense of low self-worth and a recurring difficulty in close relationships. Her presentation is consistent with persistent depressive disorder (F34.1), a low-grade but durable depressive pattern that predates the bereavement. From a psychodynamic perspective, the loss has reopened much older material: the grief is not only for her mother but for the relationship she wished she had and never quite received. Her chronic sense of [[Worthlessness]] appears rooted in early experiences of feeling criticized and unseen.

Treatment uses a psychodynamic framework, attending to the unconscious templates Eleanor brings to relationships and to the defenses (early on, marked intellectualization) that keep painful affect at a distance. The therapeutic alliance built steadily across the first sessions, which allowed her enduring [[Relational-Patterns]] to surface in the room itself. A central development has been the emergence of transference: Eleanor came to experience the therapist as critical and withholding, echoing her mother, which offered a live opportunity for interpretation.

The [[Grief-and-Loss]] work has increasingly linked her mourning to her self-worth, revealing how she organized her identity around earning an approval that never reliably came. Transference interpretation has been offered and worked with, and Eleanor's insight into her relational template is consolidating. There are tentative but real shifts in her outside relationships as the working-through continues. No risk concerns have been identified; risk was screened at intake and again when grief material intensified, and she denied ideation on both occasions.
