## Data
Eleanor arrived composed and led with a detailed, almost lecture-like account of her family history, complete with dates and tidy explanations. She described her mother's criticism in measured, analytic terms and reported the homework observations as data points rather than felt experiences. When I asked how a recent slight from a colleague had felt, she answered with a theory about the colleague's personality instead of an emotion.

## Assessment
This session brought her defenses into clearer view. The dominant defense is intellectualization: Eleanor manages threatening affect by converting it into ideas and analysis, keeping vulnerability at a safe remove. Within a psychodynamic frame, this is the same maneuver that protects her from the disappointment underneath her [[Worthlessness]] and sustains her guarded [[Relational-Patterns]]. I named the pattern gently, using early [[transference-interpretation]] to observe how she was doing it with me too, "giving me the analysis so I would not see the ache." She smiled and said, "If I explain it well enough, maybe it will not hurt." That was a genuine and specific response, a small crack in the defense and a step toward Goal 3. The barrier is that intellectualization is ego-syntonic and long-practiced, so it will need patient, repeated attention rather than confrontation.

## Plan
Continue building the alliance while gently drawing attention to intellectualization as it operates in the room. Homework: when she notices herself explaining a feeling, pause and name the feeling itself.

## Next Session Considerations
- Consider inviting grief material more directly.
- Possible focus on the disappointment beneath the analysis.
