## Data
Eleanor came in subdued, having spent the weekend sorting her mother's belongings. For the first time the analytic distance thinned. She described finding a birthday card she had made as a child and being flooded, "I kept it my whole life and she never once said she liked it." She cried briefly, then apologized for it. Because the grief material intensified, risk was screened again: she denied any suicidal or self-harm ideation, intent, or plan, and said the sadness felt "heavy but survivable."

## Assessment
The [[Grief-and-Loss]] here is layered. Eleanor is mourning her mother and, underneath, mourning the approval she never reliably received. Through a psychodynamic lens, this is where her [[Worthlessness]] was forged: her value became contingent on earning a recognition that rarely came, and her mother's death forecloses any hope of finally getting it. I linked the grief to her self-worth explicitly, and when she began to intellectualize again I used [[transference-interpretation]] to note that she had just apologized to me for crying, "as if you expected me to be unimpressed, the way she was." She went still and said, "I do assume you are judging the mess." That specific response is important early evidence of the transference forming, and it marks real progress toward Goal 2 as feeling began to replace analysis.

## Plan
Stay with the grief and its link to self-worth; support affect tolerance rather than resolution. Homework: let herself feel one grief wave this week without explaining it away.

## Next Session Considerations
- Consider following the assumption that others, including me, are judging her.
- Possible focus on the unmet longing inside the mourning.

Clinical judgment and final session planning remain the therapist's responsibility.
