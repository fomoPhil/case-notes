## Data
Eleanor arrived noticeably cooler and more clipped. Early on she referenced my having glanced at the clock the previous session and said, flatly, "You were probably bored with me." Through the hour she was watchful, quick to read disapproval into my brief silences, and at one point said she assumed I thought she was "wallowing" in her grief.

## Assessment
The transference has emerged in force. Eleanor is now experiencing me as critical, withholding, and easily disappointed, a direct echo of her mother, and she is relating to me from inside that expectation. This is a live enactment of her [[Relational-Patterns]] and the engine of her [[Worthlessness]]: she pre-empts anticipated criticism by withdrawing first. I noticed my own countertransference, a faint pull to reassure her and prove I was not bored, which I understood as the complementary role her template recruits in others. Rather than reassure, I stayed with the material and offered a tentative [[transference-interpretation]], observing that she seemed braced for me to find her wanting exactly as she had been found wanting before. She resisted at first, saying "I am just reading the room," but then conceded, "Maybe I am waiting for you to prove me right." That specific response marks a barrier partly giving way and is meaningful progress toward Goal 1.

## Plan
Hold the transference in view without collapsing it through reassurance; use my countertransference as information. Homework: notice where else this week she assumes others are disappointed in her.

## Next Session Considerations
- Consider a fuller interpretation linking the in-room experience to her mother.
- Possible focus on what she fears will happen if she stops bracing.
