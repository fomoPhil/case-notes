Thomas is a 38-year-old single man who sought treatment following a serious motor vehicle accident that left him with post-traumatic symptoms. He presented with intrusive memories of the collision, sleep disruption, and marked hypervigilance while driving, which had begun to constrict his daily routine. His presentation meets criteria for post-traumatic stress disorder (F43.10) arising from a single incident. Treatment uses EMDR, working through the standard eight phases to reprocess the target memory of the accident. Central to his work is [[Trauma-Reprocessing]], with the collision as the primary target for [[bilateral-stimulation]].

Early sessions covered history taking, then preparation and resourcing, where Thomas installed a calm place and other stabilizing resources before reprocessing began. His baseline distress on the target memory was high, with a Subjective Units of Disturbance (SUDs) rating of 8 out of 10 and a Validity of Cognition (VOC) rating of 2 out of 7 for the positive cognition "I am safe now." The negative cognition driving his distress was "I am not safe" and, at times, "I am powerless." His symptoms clustered around [[Anxiety]] and a pattern of [[Avoidance-and-Approach]] that showed most clearly in his driving.

Across the reprocessing phases his ratings moved steadily and cleanly. SUDs fell from 8 to 6, then 4, then 2, then 1, and finally to 0, while VOC rose from 2 through 3, 4, 5, 6, and reached a full 7. The final session completed desensitization, installation, and a clean body scan, with reevaluation confirming durable gains. Thomas reported that the improvements were generalizing, driving with noticeably less hypervigilance and re-engaging with routes he had been avoiding. He remains stable, motivated, and free of active risk indicators.
