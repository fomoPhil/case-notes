## Data
Priya arrived with a mostly completed diary card, her first full week of tracking. It showed self-harm urges rated 3 to 4 on two evenings, both after conflict with a housemate, and low skills use overall. She described one evening where the urge felt "like the only exit," though she did not act on it. She linked the spikes to feeling criticized, a recurring driver of her [[Emotional-Dysregulation]] and [[Distress-Intolerance]].

## Assessment
The diary card gives us a clear behavioral picture: urges cluster around interpersonal friction and peak when she has no skill in place. I introduced [[chain-analysis]] and we walked through the Tuesday urge link by link, from the prompting event (a terse text) through the vulnerability (poor sleep), interpreting thoughts, the emotion surge, and the urge itself. She was struck by seeing the gap between event and action, noting, "There were about five points where I could have done something different." Identifying those links is direct progress toward Goal 1, giving her intervention targets rather than a single overwhelming moment.

## Plan
Continue daily [[diary-card]] entries with attention to sleep as a vulnerability factor. Homework: attempt one chain analysis independently if an urge arises. Preview distress tolerance skills for next session.

## Next Session Considerations
- Consider introducing TIPP as a first distress tolerance skill.
- Possible focus on sleep and routine as vulnerability reduction.

Clinical judgment and final session planning remain the therapist's responsibility.
