## Data
Priya presented in acute crisis. Two days earlier her partner ended the relationship abruptly, and she came in tearful, agitated, and describing a night of overwhelming distress. She reported active thoughts of not wanting to be alive and a strong self-harm urge the previous evening that she resisted. This is her sharpest [[Relational-Patterns]] trigger colliding with her [[Emotional-Dysregulation]] and [[Distress-Intolerance]], with the breakup experienced as total abandonment. She had not acted on the urge but felt frightened by its intensity.

## Assessment
This is a high-risk presentation of active suicidal ideation with some intent, in the context of an interpersonal loss. The full risk assessment is documented below. Clinically, her emotional vulnerability was maximal: little sleep, not eating, and a flood of shame and abandonment terror. We used [[chain-analysis]] on the previous night's urge to slow the experience down and locate intervention points, and rehearsed [[distress-tolerance-skills]] in the room. She was able to complete paced breathing and the temperature technique and reported her distress dropping from a 5 to a 3, saying, "I can breathe now, I did not think I could get it down." Her ability to use the skills under acute distress is a protective sign and progress on Goal 2, even in crisis.

## Plan
Create and provide a written safety plan (see Risk). Shorten the interval to a follow-up within days. Homework limited to safety plan use and daily contact with her sister. She agreed to call the crisis line or attend the emergency department if the urge returned with intent, and to contact between sessions if needed.

## Risk
- Assessed: Yes
- Ideation: Active suicidal ideation reported, "I keep thinking it would be easier not to be here," present since the breakup two days ago and most intense at night.
- Plan, intent, means: A fleeting plan was considered (an overdose of over-the-counter medication) but not developed or rehearsed. She reported some intent at the peak of distress but stated she does not want to die so much as wants the pain to stop, and intent was absent at the time of session. No means were secured or prepared. Means-restriction counseling was completed: she agreed to give the tablets in her flat to her sister for safekeeping tonight, and a recent self-harm urge involved no acquired means.
- Protective factors: Strong bond with her sister, whom she sees as a reason for living, active engagement in therapy and skills, no history of a medically serious attempt, ambivalence weighted toward living, and future orientation toward finishing her studies.
- Interventions taken: A collaborative written safety plan was created together, covering personal warning signs, internal coping strategies (TIPP, self-soothe), people and settings that distract, people to ask for help, professional and crisis contacts, and making the environment safe. The crisis line number and local emergency numbers were reviewed and written into the plan. Means restriction was agreed as above. A [[chain-analysis]] of the urge was completed and [[distress-tolerance-skills]] were rehearsed to a reduction in distress. A follow-up was scheduled within four days and she agreed to contact between sessions if the urge intensified.
