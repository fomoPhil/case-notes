## Data
Priya reported a calmer fortnight with urges rated no higher than 2. Risk was screened: she denied suicidal ideation, intent, and plan, and denied any acute self-harm urge, though she noted baseline urges are still tracked on the card. She described one moment of rising distress at work that she "sat through instead of running from."

## Assessment
The presentation continues to reflect [[Distress-Intolerance]], where the felt sense is that intense emotion must be discharged immediately. I taught the [[distress-tolerance-skills]] set, focusing on TIPP (temperature, intense exercise, paced breathing, paired muscle relaxation) as a fast physiological down-regulator for crisis moments. We practiced paced breathing and the temperature technique in session. She noticed her shoulders drop and said, "Oh, that is actually different, my body believed it." This concrete in-session shift is meaningful progress toward Goal 2, giving her a survival skill for the peak of [[Emotional-Dysregulation]] rather than relying on urges.

## Plan
Add a TIPP rehearsal to the daily [[diary-card]] routine so the skill is practiced before it is needed. Homework: use TIPP once when distress reaches a 3 and log the outcome.
