## Data
Jane returned having kept her sleep-onset log for two weeks. She reported onset still averaging around 70 minutes but noted a clearer pattern: the longest delays followed evenings spent scrolling news and work email in bed. She connected this to her [[Anxiety]], observing that screens "feed the checklist." Her values reflection was partial but sincere; she said the exercise "made me notice I keep waiting to feel calm before I let myself rest."

## Assessment
Jane is beginning to see worry as a mental event rather than a command. To build on this, I introduced [[cognitive-defusion]], teaching the "I am having the thought that..." labeling technique and a brief leaves-on-a-stream visualization for bedtime rumination. In session she practiced with a live worry about an upcoming presentation and reported the thought "felt less like a wall and more like weather passing." That shift is an early but concrete response to the intervention. We revisited [[values-clarification]] to tie a screen-free wind-down to her value of focused, rested work rather than to mere symptom reduction, supporting the sleep goal. Progress toward the willingness goal is emerging, though [[Sleep]] onset remains the practical barrier.

## Plan
Homework: practice defusion labeling nightly when worry appears, continue the sleep log, and begin a 30-minute screen-free window before bed. Carry forward the presentation as a values-guided approach opportunity.

## Next Session Considerations
- Consider deepening willingness work around anxious bodily sensations.
- Possible focus on reviewing the screen-free window's effect on onset.
