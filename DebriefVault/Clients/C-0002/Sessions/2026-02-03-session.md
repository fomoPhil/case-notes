## Data
Intake session. Jane, self-referred, described roughly eight months of persistent [[Anxiety]] centered on anticipatory worry about the next day, alongside disrupted [[Sleep]] with delayed onset she estimated at 60 to 90 minutes most nights. She reported lying awake "running through tomorrow like a checklist that never ends." She denied panic attacks but described chest tightness and racing thoughts at bedtime. Risk was screened directly: she denied any suicidal or self-harm ideation, intent, or plan, and reported strong reasons for living including her work and close friendships. Mood was anxious but engaged; she was articulate and motivated.

## Assessment
Presentation is consistent with generalized anxiety (F41.9). Through an ACT lens, the core process appears to be experiential avoidance: Jane treats worry as a problem to solve rather than an experience to hold, which amplifies arousal at exactly the time she needs to wind down. I introduced brief values clarification, using [[values-clarification]] to explore what a rested, present version of her life would serve. She named "being a steady friend and doing focused work" as anchors, and noticed aloud, "I have been organizing my whole evening around not feeling anxious." That recognition landed as a useful reframe and opens the door to willingness work.

## Plan
Begin establishing the treatment-plan goal of increasing willingness to experience anxiety without avoidance. Homework: a short daily values reflection and a simple sleep-onset log. Psychoeducation on ACT provided.

## Next Session Considerations
- Consider introducing a defusion exercise for bedtime worry.
- Possible focus on mapping current avoidance behaviors.
