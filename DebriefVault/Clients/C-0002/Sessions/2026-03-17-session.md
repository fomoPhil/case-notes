## Data
Jane reported the defusion practice is becoming more automatic. She delivered her presentation and, though anxious beforehand, said she "let the nerves ride along instead of canceling." Her sleep log showed onset improving on nights she held the screen-free window, down to roughly 45 to 55 minutes, but she admitted skipping the window three times when work ran late. Bedtime [[Anxiety]] still spikes as physical restlessness.

## Assessment
Jane's [[cognitive-defusion]] skills are consolidating, so I shifted emphasis toward [[willingness-and-acceptance]], reframing the goal from quieting anxious sensations to making room for them. We ran a brief interoceptive willingness exercise: she attended to chest tightness for two minutes without trying to fix it and rated her urge to distract before and after. She noted the urge dropped from an 8 to a 4 and remarked, "I did not know I could just let it be there." This is a meaningful response and directly serves the willingness goal. The barrier remains behavioral consistency: late work nights collapse the wind-down routine, which is where her committed action needs reinforcement to protect [[Sleep]].

## Plan
Homework: a nightly two-minute willingness practice with anxious sensations, continue defusion as needed, and problem-solve a firm cutoff time for work so the screen-free window holds. Continue logging onset.

## Next Session Considerations
- Consider formalizing committed action around a fixed work cutoff.
- Possible focus on self-as-context to hold anxiety more lightly.

Clinical judgment and final session planning remain the therapist's responsibility.
