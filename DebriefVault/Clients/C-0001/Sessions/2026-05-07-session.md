## Data
Bob brought a completed hierarchy of feedback situations, ranked from asking a colleague for informal input (least threatening) to his annual performance review (most threatening). He had already attempted the lowest item, asking a peer to critique a draft, and reported that the peer gave two minor suggestions and moved on. "The world did not end," he said. Risk was explicitly screened this session and Bob denied any thoughts of self-harm, suicide, or wishing he were not alive. Mood and sleep continued to improve.

## Assessment
This session formally began [[graded-exposure]] to workplace feedback, the mechanism most likely to disconfirm the [[Work-Undermining]] belief that evaluation equals exposure of inadequacy. His first exposure produced habituation and a concrete disconfirming outcome. I used [[socratic-questioning]] to consolidate what the exercise taught him about the gap between predicted and actual threat, targeting the [[Perfectionism]]-driven fear of any critique. Belief conviction for "I am worthless" was 60 percent, half its intake level. His willingness to seek feedback voluntarily is a marked behavioral change and strong progress on Goal 1. The barrier is the steep jump to higher-hierarchy items.

## Plan
Assigned the next exposure step: proactively asking his manager for feedback on one task. Continue thought records and value-based activities.

## Next Session Considerations
- Consider reviewing the manager-feedback exposure in detail.
- Possible focus on building tolerance for uncertainty before the annual review.
