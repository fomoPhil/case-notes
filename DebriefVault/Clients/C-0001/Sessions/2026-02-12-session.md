## Data
Bob completed both thought records and, for the first time, filled in the balanced-alternative column without prompting. He brought a situation where he had stayed late rewriting a report three times because "it was not good enough to send." He described a rule that anything less than flawless would expose him. He connected this to [[Perfectionism]] and to a childhood message that only top performance earned approval. Mood was slightly improved; he reported one genuinely relaxed weekend.

## Assessment
The session clarified how perfectionist standards drive the [[Work-Undermining]] cycle: impossibly high bars guarantee perceived failure, which reinforces worthlessness. Belief conviction for "I am worthless" was 85 percent. I used [[cognitive-restructuring]] to test the prediction that a "merely good" report would be criticized. Bob agreed to send a report he judged 80 percent polished rather than reworking it. He said afterward, "I felt sick pressing send, but nothing bad happened, and my manager thanked me." That disconfirming experience directly advances Goal 1 by weakening the link between imperfection and catastrophe. The barrier remains his discomfort tolerance in the moment of letting work go.

## Plan
Continue [[thought-records]], adding a column rating how accurate each catastrophic prediction turned out to be. Set an experiment to submit one piece of work without over-checking it.

## Next Session Considerations
- Consider building a graded list of performance situations by difficulty.
- Possible focus on identifying value-based activities outside work.
