## Data
Intake session. Bob is a mid-career professional who described mounting workplace stress over the past six months, centered on a belief that colleagues view his output as substandard. He reported low mood, difficulty concentrating, and a pattern of replaying meetings looking for evidence he had failed. He linked the stress directly to [[Worthlessness]], stating "if I slip at work, there is nothing left that makes me worth anything." Sleep and appetite were mildly disrupted but not severely. Risk was screened explicitly at intake and Bob denied any current or historical thoughts of self-harm, suicide, or wanting to be dead. He presented as articulate, cooperative, and visibly relieved to be describing the pattern out loud.

## Assessment
Presentation is consistent with generalized anxiety (F41.1) with a prominent depressive-cognitive component. The core belief of worthlessness is fused to work performance, producing a fragile self-concept vulnerable to [[Work-Undermining]] appraisals. Initial belief-conviction rating for "I am worthless" was 90 percent. I introduced the CBT model, mapping the link between situations, automatic thoughts, and mood. I began [[cognitive-restructuring]] by gently separating a specific event from his global self-judgment. Bob responded, "I never noticed I do that jump," which suggested good capacity for metacognitive distance. This engagement supports early progress on Goal 1 (reduce self-critical automatic thoughts).

## Plan
Assigned a first [[thought-records]] worksheet to capture one work stressor before the next session. Provided psychoeducation handout on the cognitive triangle. Agreed to biweekly cadence.
