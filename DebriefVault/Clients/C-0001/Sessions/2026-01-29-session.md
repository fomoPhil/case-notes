## Data
Bob arrived having completed his first thought record after a project review where his manager requested a revision. He recorded the automatic thought "he thinks I am incompetent" and rated his mood at the time as low. He described spending the evening rereading old emails for proof of failure. He noted, with some surprise, that writing the thought down "made it look smaller on the page than it felt in my head." Mood over the fortnight was variable but he attended work every day and met his deadlines.

## Assessment
The completed [[thought-records]] revealed two recurring distortions: mind reading and catastrophizing, both feeding the [[Worthlessness]] belief. Belief conviction for "I am worthless" was 88 percent, essentially stable, which is expected this early. I used [[cognitive-restructuring]] to examine the evidence for and against the interpretation that the revision request meant incompetence. When I asked what he would tell a colleague in the same position, Bob paused and said, "I would tell them a revision is normal, obviously." This shift in perspective, applying a fairer standard to himself, marks concrete movement on Goal 1.

## Plan
Assigned two thought records this fortnight, prompting Bob to complete the balanced-alternative column himself rather than leaving it blank. Encouraged him to notice the mind-reading pattern in real time.

## Next Session Considerations
- Consider introducing a hierarchy of the situations that trigger the strongest self-criticism.
- Possible focus on how perfectionist standards shape his interpretations.
