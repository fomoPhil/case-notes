## Data
Bob arrived tired after a demanding fortnight but had scheduled two non-work activities: a Saturday hike and a long-postponed dinner with an old friend. He rated his mood before and after each. Both rose noticeably afterward, contradicting his prediction that he would "feel like a fraud enjoying himself while behind on work." Risk was explicitly screened this session and Bob denied any thoughts of self-harm, suicide, or feeling that others would be better off without him. He described the friend dinner as "the first time in ages I felt like a person, not a job title."

## Assessment
This session introduced [[behavioral-activation]] to test whether worth could be experienced outside performance, directly targeting the [[Worthlessness]] belief. The mood ratings provided quantitative disconfirmation. Belief conviction for "I am worthless" fell to 78 percent. I paired this with [[cognitive-restructuring]] of the prediction that rest equals failure. His statement about feeling like "a person, not a job title" is a specific, values-linked response and represents clear progress on Goal 2 (rebuild worth independent of work). The barrier is guilt, which still attaches to any time not spent working.

## Plan
Continue activity scheduling with two value-based activities per fortnight, logging mood before and after. Maintain thought records for work stressors.

## Next Session Considerations
- Consider using guided questioning to examine the guilt that follows rest.
- Possible focus on widening the range of scheduled activities.

Clinical judgment and final session planning remain the therapist's responsibility.
