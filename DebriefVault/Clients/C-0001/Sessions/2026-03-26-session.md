## Data
Bob completed all scheduled activities and continued logging mood. He raised a difficult moment: a peer was praised in a team meeting and he immediately concluded he was "invisible and replaceable." He noticed the thought, wrote it down, but struggled to shift it in the moment. He reported his sleep had improved and he was ruminating less in the evenings.

## Assessment
I used [[socratic-questioning]] to unpack the meaning he attached to a colleague's praise, asking what it would mean if two people could both be valued at once. Bob worked through it aloud and arrived at, "I suppose praise is not a fixed pie." This is a genuine cognitive shift generated by his own reasoning rather than supplied by me, which strengthens durability. The episode illustrates how comparison fuels the [[Work-Undermining]] and [[Worthlessness]] loop. Belief conviction for "I am worthless" dropped to 72 percent. Continued [[behavioral-activation]] is stabilizing mood between sessions, supporting both treatment goals. The barrier is the speed of the automatic comparison, which still fires before he can intervene.

## Plan
Assigned a thought record specifically for comparison situations, using Socratic prompts on the worksheet. Continue two value-based activities per fortnight.

## Next Session Considerations
- Consider rehearsing a brief in-the-moment coping response for comparison triggers.
- Possible focus on the origins of the "replaceable" belief.

Clinical judgment and final session planning remain the therapist's responsibility.
